How to use
  • Give the whole strip the first row's seconds. Then cover the first patch and give the rest what the next row says under Add, cover the next patch, and so on. Seconds is what a patch has had by then.
  • If you would rather expose every patch on its own, give each what its row says under Seconds.
  • No timer? Choose "a metronome" above and the table turns into beats to count, or "this page's sound" and the page sounds every moment you have to act on.
  • Set any metronome, a physical one or an app, to the tempo shown. If it can accent beats, let it accent the one you count.
  • Start the exposure on the count of zero. Keep counting, and cover the next patch of the strip at each count in the table.
  • Step error is how far a row's real exposure lands from its target, as a share of one step.
  • Ticking "Count each step from zero" restarts the count for every patch. This can be easier to count when you want to stop exposing between each patch.
  • If your metronome lacks the tempo shown, press "My metronome can't do …". The next best tempo appears, and the choice is remembered on this device.
  • There is purposely no metronome built in: this way of timing exists for the accuracy a dedicated metronome gives, and finding the tempo is the whole trick. If you would rather the page did the timing, that is what "this page's sound" above is for, and if what you want is a proper f-stop timer, something like GoTimer's enlarger timer is a better fit.
  • The page times the run itself. Open Darkroom view below the table, then Start. The count-in before the lamp goes on is set under "Count-in and placing the base", and is remembered: make it as long as you need to get a hand to the switch.
  • Under the count-in a drone sounds an octave below the one that follows it, so the two can never be confused; on the lamp it steps up, and holds for as long as the exposure runs. Over it a second tone climbs, and arrives an octave higher exactly on each mark, where a short accent lands: one for each patch to cover, and the last is the lamp off. Silence means it is done.
  • There are two ways to start it. Start keeps the red type on screen as the run goes. Start dark gives the screen up altogether: there is nothing to read once you are going by ear. It stays black after the run ends, too, rather than lighting the room while the paper is still out.
  • On that black screen the whole of it is the one control. A tap starts a run, and stops one that is going; a hold gives the screen back and ends the run with it.
  • Started the lit way, a run can only be stopped by holding the button until the sound ends. A quick touch does nothing, so that a hand brushing the phone cannot spoil a strip.
  • On an iPhone this may pause music playing from another app. Bluetooth speakers and headphones delay every sound by the same amount, which does no harm when you go by ear.
  • To see how far apart the patches will be, look at the strip under each row. It shows roughly what that patch prints as, in the zones of the Zone System: 0 is full black, V middle grey and X paper white, on the paper and at the filter set above the table. Your prints may differ.
  • To see what a tone does in the other patches, find it in the boxed row, the reference, and look straight up or down: a place along the strips is one part of the picture. Tap or click the row you take to be right to make it the reference.
  • To follow one tone closely, slide a finger sideways along a row, or drag with the mouse: every strip then shows that part of the picture in full, and the zone it is nearest to. The row you slide on becomes the reference.
  • To see what another filter would do, open "Compare with another filter" and set it. Every row gets a second strip under its first: the same seconds through that filter. Follow a tone from the boxed row's upper strip straight down as before, and the row whose lower strip has it right gives the seconds for that filter. This goes by Ilford's speeds for the paper: filters 4 and 5 need a stop more light than 00 to 3 on Multigrade IV, and an eighth of a stop more on Multigrade RC Deluxe. Your enlarger may differ.
  • Darkroom view turns the page into large red type on black, to read at the enlarger. It shows only what your hands need, the plan or the Start button, and nothing that can be set. Escape, or Close, brings the page back.
  • Red only is safer than white, not safe. The screen is still a light source, and Multigrade paper is most sensitive to the green and blue in it. Turn the phone's brightness right down and keep it away from the paper.
  • Test your own phone before you trust it: leave a scrap of paper face up beside the phone showing the view for five minutes, then develop it.
  • Turn on airplane mode, or Do Not Disturb, before you start. A notification or a call lights the whole screen.
  • Open the page once while online, and after that it works in airplane mode.
  • To keep it to hand, add it to the home screen: on iPhone from Share → Add to Home Screen, on Android from the browser menu → Install app, or Add to home screen. On an iPhone that is the only way to be rid of the browser's own bars in the darkroom view.
  • Everything is calculated in your browser. Nothing is sent anywhere.
