How to use
  • Type in the exposure you think the print needs, how big a step to take between patches, and how many patches. The map then has a row for each patch: its seconds, large, and under them how many stops it is from the base.
  • Your guess is the base. It starts on the middle patch, so the others bracket it; tap a patch, its figure or its strip, to make that one the base instead — on the first, the strip only goes up from there.
  • Every patch has two figures: what it has had in all, and what it adds to the one before. The switch over them, Totals or Additions, says which a run gives, and that column is in ink: totals for a strip moved along in a holder, each patch exposed on its own; additions for a strip covered as it goes, the next patch covered at each figure. With a timer, that is all there is to it.
  • The strip beside each figure shows roughly what that patch prints as, in the zones of the Zone System: 0 is full black, V middle grey and X paper white, on the paper and at the filter set under the map. The base's strip is the whole scale, and every other is that scale after its patch's exposure. Your prints may differ.
  • To see what a tone does in the other patches, find it in the base's strip, the boxed one, and look straight up or down: a place across the map is one part of the picture.
  • To follow one tone closely, slide a finger sideways along any strip, or drag with the mouse: every strip then shows that part of the picture in full, and the zone it is nearest to. Sliding picks nothing; the line stays where you leave it.
  • To see what another filter would do, open "Compare with another filter" and set it. Every row gets a second strip under its first: the same seconds through that filter. Follow a tone from the base's upper strip straight down as before, and the patch whose lower strip has it right gives the seconds for that filter. This goes by Ilford's speeds for the paper: filters 4 and 5 need a stop more light than 00 to 3 on Multigrade IV and Art 300, and an eighth of a stop more on FB Classic and Multigrade RC Deluxe. Your enlarger may differ.
  • The page can keep the time itself, sounding every moment you have to act on: press "Run in Darkroom Mode" at the foot of the screen. If you would rather count on a metronome, tick "Use a metronome instead" and the figures turn into counts. Both ways have a fold at the end of this list.
  • The darkroom view is large red type on black, to read at the enlarger. It shows only what your hands need, the plan and the Start button, and nothing that can be set. Escape, or Close, brings the page back.
  • Red only is safer than white, not safe. The screen is still a light source, and Multigrade paper is most sensitive to the green and blue in it. Turn the phone's brightness right down and keep it away from the paper.
  • Test your own phone before you trust it: leave a scrap of paper face up beside the phone showing the view for five minutes, then develop it.
  • Turn on airplane mode, or Do Not Disturb, before you start. A notification or a call lights the whole screen.
  • Open the page once while online, and after that it works in airplane mode.
  • To keep it to hand, add it to the home screen: on iPhone from Share → Add to Home Screen, on Android from the browser menu → Install app, or Add to home screen. On an iPhone that is the only way to be rid of the browser's own bars in the darkroom view.
  • Everything is calculated in your browser. Nothing is sent anywhere.
In the darkroom
  • Press "Run in Darkroom Mode", then Start. The plan is the map's figures with a bar beside each, as long as that exposure, and one above them for the count-in: the seconds the page waits before the lamp goes on, set under Configuration and remembered. Make it as long as you need to get a hand to the switch.
  • A run gives every patch exactly the figure beside it, in turn: with Additions what it adds, the next patch covered at each mark; with Totals its whole exposure, one after the other, the strip moved along at each mark.
  • The count-in counts three beats in, one a second, the last of them a second before the lamp. On the lamp a drone comes in and holds for as long as the exposure runs. Over it a tone climbs, and arrives an octave higher exactly on each mark, where a short beat lands, louder and an octave above the ones that counted you in: one for each patch, and the last is the lamp off. Silence means it is done. As the run goes, the bar of the exposure it is on fills, and the figures stay the brightest thing on the screen.
  • There are two ways to start it. Start keeps the red type on screen as the run goes. Start dark gives the screen up altogether: there is nothing to read once you are going by ear. It stays black after the run ends, too, rather than lighting the room while the paper is still out.
  • On that black screen the whole of it is the one control. A tap starts a run, and stops one that is going; a hold gives the screen back and ends the run with it.
  • Started the lit way, a press on the button stops the run at once: there is a screen to see it on, and the hold belongs to the dark. If a call or another app takes the page away, the run stops and the view says so.
  • With "Stop between exposures" ticked, Start runs one patch at a time. It says which first — one short blip for the first patch, two for the second — then counts in, gives the patch its figure, and the lamp goes off. The view then waits, and Resume, or a tap on the black screen, runs the next; Reset goes back to the first patch. A patch stopped part way starts again from its blips.
  • On an iPhone this may pause music playing from another app. Bluetooth speakers and headphones delay every sound by the same amount, which does no harm when you go by ear.
  • With "Use a metronome instead" ticked, Start runs a metronome at the tempo shown rather than the strip, for as long as you let it, the beat you count struck an octave above the rest; Start dark, the tap and the hold work as they do for a run.
Your own metronome
  • Tick "Use a metronome instead". The page then says what tempo to set and which beats to count, and every figure is a count, chosen to land as close to the f-stop time as a tempo allows. A count of 1⅔ is the second beat after the first counted one.
  • Set any metronome, a physical one or an app, to that tempo. If it can accent beats, let it accent the one you count. Or let the page keep it: in the darkroom view, Start runs a metronome at that tempo.
  • Start the exposure on the count of zero, and cover the next patch at each count on the map. Additions start the count again from zero at every patch; Totals keep one count running from the start of the strip.
  • When a patch lands more than a tenth of a step from its target, the map says how far beside its stops.
  • If your metronome lacks the tempo shown, press "My metronome can't do …". The next best tempo appears, and the choice is remembered on this device, so after a few sessions the page knows your metronome. A tempo skipped by mistake is brought back by pressing it under "Skipping", and "Forget all skipped tempos" under Configuration starts over.
  • Configuration also holds the tempos to search between, and "Count every", normally chosen so that you count about once a second.
  • This way of working exists for the accuracy a steady beat gives, and finding the tempo is the whole trick; the metronome the page runs is that beat and nothing more. If you would rather the page did the timing, that is what it does with the box left unticked, and if what you want is a proper f-stop timer, something like GoTimer's enlarger timer is a better fit.
